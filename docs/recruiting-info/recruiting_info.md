Recruiting


NCAA member schools have adopted rules to create an equitable recruiting environment that promotes student-athlete well-being. The rules define who may be involved in the recruiting process, when recruiting may occur and the conditions under which recruiting may be conducted. Recruiting rules seek, as much as possible, to control intrusions into the lives of student-athletes.

The NCAA defines recruiting as “any solicitation of prospective student-athletes or their parents by an institutional staff member or by a representative of the institution’s athletics interests for the purpose of securing a prospective student-athlete’s enrollment and ultimate participation in the institution’s intercollegiate athletics program.”

What is a contact?

A contact occurs any time a college coach says more than hello during a face-to-face contact with a college-bound student-athlete or their parents off the college’s campus.

What is a contact period?
During a contact period, college coaches may:

Call, write, text or email you and your family.
Watch you compete.
Visit your high school.
Have face-to-face contact with you and your family.
Note: Keep in mind that certain recruiting activities, such as calls, visits to your high school, face-to-face contact and other forms of correspondence, may have division- and sport-specific restrictions before certain grade levels in high school.

What is an evaluation period?
During an evaluation period, college coaches may:

Call, write, text or email you and your family.
Watch you compete.
Visit your high school.
Have face-to-face contact with you and your family, but only on the college's campus.
Note: Keep in mind that certain recruiting activities, such as calls, visits to your high school, face-to-face contact and other forms of correspondence, may have division- and sport-specific restrictions before certain grade levels in high school.

What is a quiet period?
During a quiet period, college coaches may:

Call, write, text or email you and your family.
Have face-to-face contact with you and your family, but only on the college's campus.
During a quiet period, college coaches may not:

Watch you compete.
Visit your high school.
Note: Keep in mind that certain recruiting activities, such as calls, visits to your high school, face-to-face contact and other forms of correspondence, may have division- and sport-specific restrictions before certain grade levels in high school.

What is a dead period?
During a dead period, college coaches may:

Call, write, text or email you and your family.
During a dead period, college coaches may not:

Watch you compete.
Visit your high school.
Have face-to-face contact with you and your family.
Note: Keep in mind that certain recruiting activities, such as calls, visits to your high school, face-to-face contact and other forms of correspondence, may have division- and sport-specific restrictions before certain grade levels in high school.

Key D3 Recruiting Rules & Calendar
Recruiting Materials: Coaches can send brochures, questionnaires, and other materials at any time.
Phone Calls/Digital Communication: There are no restrictions on the timing or frequency of phone calls, emails, and text messages.
Off-Campus Contact: Coaches may begin meeting with recruits and their families off-campus (e.g., at high school or home) after the athlete’s sophomore year.
Official Visits: Prospects can take official visits (one per school) starting January 1 of their junior year.
Unofficial Visits: Athletes can take an unlimited number of unofficial visits to campus at any time.
Evaluations: No restrictions on the number of times a coach can evaluate a student-athlete.
Tryouts: D3 schools are generally prohibited from conducting, evaluating, or arranging on-campus or off-campus tryouts/tests for recruits.
Signing Day: D3 coaches are not permitted to attend high school signing day celebrations.

Key NJCAA Recruiting Rules & Guidelines
Contact Timing: No regulated calendar; coaches can call, text, or email recruits at any time, unlike the strict NCAA rules.
Official Visits: Paid campus visits can begin after the prospect completes their junior year.
Visit Duration & Scope: Visits cannot exceed two days and two nights, restricted to the campus and local area.
Off-Campus Contact: Allowed, but usually, coaches work within smaller, personal networks due to smaller budgets compared to NCAA.
Signing Letters of Intent: If a student signs an NJCAA Letter of Intent, all other NJCAA institutions must cease recruitment.
Meals: Coaches may pay for meals during an on-campus visit.
